## story {transition=cut hold=1.2}
[loud]What if every school in India taught in just one language?[/loud] [#p1] India has 22 official languages. The {2011|twenty eleven} census counted 121 languages spoken by more than 10,000 people each. [#p2] Now picture a six year old from a Santali, Tulu or Bhojpuri home walking into a class where everything is in a language they've barely heard. [#p3] Research, including work by UNESCO, says children learn best in the language they speak at home. India's own new education policy suggests teaching in the mother tongue until at least class 5. [#p4] There is an upside. One shared language could let a student from Tamil Nadu study, work and move anywhere in India. Textbooks and exams could be made once. [#p5] But language is also identity. In 1965, anti-Hindi protests in Tamil Nadu turned deadly. Schooling and language have been tied to politics ever since. [#end] A shared language can connect a country, or leave millions behind. So what if all Indian schools taught in one language?
