# What if India had no caste surnames?

> Facts: the Self-Respect Movement in Tamil Nadu, 1920s and 1930s, urged people to drop caste surnames. Field studies of identical job applications found fewer callbacks for names signalling a lower caste (Thorat and Attewell, 2007, and later work). Most Indian marriages are within caste (India Human Development Survey).

## story {transition=cut hold=1.2}
[loud]What if nobody in India had a caste surname?[/loud] [#p1] In India, a surname often tells a stranger more than a name should. Caste, region, sometimes even the village. [#p2] Remove it, and the quick shortcut disappears. A landlord, an employer or a matchmaker could no longer read a name and decide. Some studies have found that identical job applications get fewer callbacks when the name signals a lower caste. [#p3] There is a real example. In Tamil Nadu, the Self-Respect Movement of the {1920s and '30s|nineteen twenties and thirties} pushed people to drop caste surnames, and many did. [#p4] But caste isn't only a name. It lives in neighbourhoods, in who eats with whom, and in marriage. The vast majority of Indians still marry within their caste. [#p5] And reservations depend on caste records. The paperwork would still have to know who is who. [#end] Taking away the label won't end the thinking behind it. But it could be a first step. So what if India had no caste surnames?
