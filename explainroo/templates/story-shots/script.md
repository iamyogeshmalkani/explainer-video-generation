# The box that changed shipping

> Facts: Maritime Executive "The Story of Malcolm McLean" and Wikipedia. McLean thought of the idea in 1937 waiting at a New Jersey pier; the Ideal X (a converted WWII tanker) sailed Newark to Houston on April 26, 1956 with 58 containers; loading cost about $5.83 a ton the old way against about 16 cents; Sea-Land's Gateway City (1957) was the first container-only ship. The cost figures cover loading only. The name is spelled "Malcom" in these sources.

## story {transition=cut hold=1.5}
[#s1] In 1956, moving cargo onto a ship cost about six dollars a ton. [#s2] Every crate, barrel and sack was loaded piece by piece. [#s3] That took time, [#s4] and time cost money. [#s5] A former truck driver named {Malcom|Mal kum} McLean had seen it himself. [#s6] In 1937, he waited most of a day at a New Jersey pier to deliver cotton bales. [#s7] He wondered why the whole truck trailer could not simply be lifted onto the ship. [#s8] Nearly twenty years later, his company converted an old tanker to carry metal boxes. [#s9] On April 26, 1956, the Ideal X sailed from Newark to Houston, [#s10] carrying fifty-eight containers. [#s11] Loading a ton now cost about sixteen cents. [#s12] The savings came from loading whole boxes instead of loose pieces. [#s13] In 1957, a ship called Gateway City became the first to carry only containers. [#s14] Today, that steel box carries goods around the world.
