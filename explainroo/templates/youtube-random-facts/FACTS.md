# 20 tech facts for youtube-random-facts videos

Each entry has the hook (the first line the voice says, in English and in Hindi), the fact, two or three supporting beats, sticker ideas
and a note on what was checked. Researched on 2026-10-26 with web searches; the "Check" line says what the sources
agreed on and what to soften. Re-check any number that changes over time (marked LIVE) the day you publish.

---

### 1. Pixar almost lost Toy Story 2
- **Hook:** "Did you know Pixar almost deleted Toy Story 2 by accident?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पिक्सार गलती से टॉय स्टोरी 2 को लगभग मिटा चुका था?"
- **Fact:** In 1998 a delete command wiped out most of the film's files from Pixar's servers.
- **Beats:** The backups had been failing too. A copy on technical director Galyn Susman's home computer saved it. The film came out in 1999.
- **Stickers:** toy cowboy, clapperboard, computer, folder, trash can, house (this is the sample in `script.md`).
- **Check:** Sources agree on the accident, the broken backups and the home copy. They disagree on the exact command (`rm -rf *` or `rm -rf /`) and on how long the backups had been failing (weeks to about a month). Say "most of the film", not an exact percentage.

### 2. Voyager 1 runs on 69 kilobytes
- **Hook:** "Did you know the farthest spacecraft from Earth runs on less memory than one photo on your phone?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पृथ्वी से सबसे दूर का अंतरिक्ष यान आपके फ़ोन की एक फ़ोटो से भी कम मेमोरी पर चलता है?"
- **Fact:** Voyager's computers hold about 69 kilobytes of memory (IEEE Spectrum: 69.63 KB).
- **Beats:** Launched in 1977, it still sends data from interstellar space. Engineers fixed a failed memory chip by sending a software patch, and a command takes about a day to arrive.
- **Stickers:** space probe, phone with a camera, memory chip, antenna dish.
- **Check:** 69 KB is the figure for the flight data subsystem computer; another source says 68 KB in total. Say "about 69 kilobytes". Leave out the distance (LIVE).

### 3. Apollo 11 had 4 kilobytes of RAM
- **Hook:** "Did you know the computer that landed humans on the Moon had about 4 kilobytes of memory?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, इंसानों को चाँद पर उतारने वाले कंप्यूटर में लगभग 4 किलोबाइट मेमोरी थी?"
- **Fact:** The Apollo Guidance Computer had 2,048 words of RAM (about 4 KB) and 36,864 words of read-only memory (about 72 KB).
- **Beats:** Its read-only memory was woven by hand from copper wire (core rope). It landed in 1969. A phone has millions of times more.
- **Stickers:** moon lander, rocket, a ball of wire, a smartphone.
- **Check:** Word counts from Wikipedia and Ken Shirriff's teardown; the kilobyte numbers are 16-bit-word arithmetic. Say "about".

### 4. The first .com domain is from 1985
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first .com website name was registered before the web even existed?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पहला .com वेबसाइट नाम वेब के आने से पहले ही रजिस्टर हो चुका था?"
- **Fact:** symbolics.com was registered on March 15, 1985, years before the first website.
- **Beats:** It belonged to a computer maker in Cambridge, Massachusetts. It was sold in 2009 and still exists.
- **Stickers:** globe, calendar, old computer, a blank browser window (use a chip for "1985").
- **Check:** Widely reported. Nuance: nordu.net was *created* on 1 January 1985, symbolics.com was the first *registered* .com.

### 5. The first webcam watched a coffee pot
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first webcam was pointed at a coffee pot?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पहला वेबकैम एक कॉफ़ी पॉट की तरफ़ लगाया गया था?"
- **Fact:** In 1991 researchers at Cambridge pointed a camera at the Trojan Room coffee pot so they would not climb the stairs to an empty pot.
- **Beats:** The picture was greyscale and updated a few times a minute. It went on the web in 1993 and was switched off in August 2001.
- **Stickers:** coffee pot, camera, stairs, a person with an empty cup.
- **Check:** Wikipedia and Cambridge/Raspberry Pi sources agree; 1991 is the camera, 1993 is the web.

### 6. The first digital camera took 23 seconds per photo
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first digital camera needed 23 seconds to save one photo?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पहले डिजिटल कैमरे को एक फ़ोटो सेव करने में 23 सेकंड लगते थे?"
- **Fact:** Kodak engineer Steven Sasson built it in 1975; the image was 100 by 100 pixels (0.01 megapixels) and went onto a cassette tape.
- **Beats:** Capturing took 50 milliseconds; recording to tape took 23 seconds. Kodak never sold it.
- **Stickers:** old bulky camera, cassette tape, a small pixel grid, a stopwatch.
- **Check:** PetaPixel, IEEE Spectrum and RPI agree.

### 7. GPS needs Einstein to work
- **Hook:** "Did you know your phone's GPS only works because of Einstein's relativity?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, आपके फ़ोन का जीपीएस आइंस्टाइन के सापेक्षता सिद्धांत की वजह से ही चलता है?"
- **Fact:** Satellite clocks run about 38 microseconds a day faster than clocks on the ground (weaker gravity adds about 45, speed removes about 7).
- **Beats:** Engineers set the clocks slightly slow before launch. Left uncorrected, the error would add up to kilometres per day.
- **Stickers:** satellite, clock, a map pin, a scientist with wild hair (an illustration, not a likeness).
- **Check:** The microsecond numbers agree across sources. The popular "11 km per day" figure is debated (receivers solve for the clock offset), so say "kilometres" only if you must, or drop it.

### 8. The internet is mostly under the sea
- **Hook:** "Did you know almost all internet traffic between continents travels through cables on the ocean floor?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, महाद्वीपों के बीच लगभग सारा इंटरनेट ट्रैफ़िक समुद्र की तलहटी में बिछी केबलों से गुज़रता है?"
- **Fact:** Submarine cables carry over 99% of intercontinental data. Satellites carry the rest.
- **Beats:** TeleGeography maps hundreds of cable systems. Ships lay and repair them.
- **Stickers:** ocean wave, cable reel, ship, shark, a continent.
- **Check:** TeleGeography calls the claim true but based on old FCC data, so say "more than 99%" or "almost all". The "thick as a garden hose" comparison is commonly quoted but was not checked; skip it unless you verify it.

### 9. The first 1 GB hard drive was the size of a fridge
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first hard drive over one gigabyte was the size of a refrigerator?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, एक गीगाबाइट से बड़ी पहली हार्ड ड्राइव एक फ़्रिज जितनी बड़ी थी?"
- **Fact:** IBM's 3380, announced in 1980, held about 2.5 GB across two units and is widely said to weigh about 250 kilograms (550 pounds).
- **Beats:** It cost well over $80,000, depending on the model. A cheap memory stick now holds more.
- **Stickers:** big metal cabinet, fridge, a tiny USB stick, a money bag.
- **Check:** The weight is widely repeated but one source suggests it refers to the full cabinet. Price varies by source ($81k to $142k). Do not quote $40,000.

### 10. The first emoji were 12 by 12 pixels
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first emoji set had 176 tiny pictures, each only 12 pixels wide?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पहले इमोजी सेट में 176 छोटी तस्वीरें थीं, और हर एक सिर्फ़ 12 पिक्सल चौड़ी थी?"
- **Fact:** Shigetaka Kurita designed 176 emoji for NTT DoCoMo's phones in 1999.
- **Beats:** Each was 12 by 12 pixels, and the whole set is about 3 kilobytes. MoMA keeps the original set. Unicode adopted emoji in 2010.
- **Stickers:** smiley, heart, sun, a pixel grid, old Japanese phone.
- **Check:** MoMA, Domus and CNN agree. One source says 1998 for the design work; use 1999 for the release.

### 11. Every top supercomputer runs Linux
- **Hook:** "Did you know the 500 fastest supercomputers in the world all run the same operating system?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, दुनिया के 500 सबसे तेज़ सुपरकंप्यूटर सब एक ही ऑपरेटिंग सिस्टम पर चलते हैं?"
- **Fact:** Since November 2017, all 500 machines on the TOP500 list run Linux.
- **Beats:** A few years earlier a handful still ran Unix.
- **Stickers:** penguin (generic, no logo), server racks, a trophy, a speedometer.
- **Check:** LIVE. Sources confirmed it through the 2020 lists; look at top500.org before publishing.

### 12. Wi-Fi does not stand for anything
- **Hook:** "Did you know Wi-Fi does not stand for 'wireless fidelity'?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, वाई-फ़ाई का मतलब "वायरलेस फ़िडेलिटी" नहीं है?"
- **Fact:** The name was made up by the branding agency Interbrand around 1999 and means nothing. It plays on "hi-fi".
- **Beats:** A later slogan, "The Standard for Wireless Fidelity", started the myth. Phil Belanger of the Wi-Fi Alliance called the slogan a mistake.
- **Stickers:** router, speaker, a question mark, a speech bubble.
- **Check:** Interbrand's case study and Belanger's statements. The year 1999 is from memory, not from the searches; confirm it or say "in the late 1990s".

### 13. The first computer mouse was made of wood
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first computer mouse was a wooden box?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पहला कंप्यूटर माउस लकड़ी का एक डिब्बा था?"
- **Fact:** Bill English built Douglas Engelbart's design in 1964: a wooden shell with wheels underneath and one button.
- **Beats:** It was shown in the "Mother of All Demos" in December 1968. The patent was granted in 1970. Nobody is sure why it is called a mouse.
- **Stickers:** wooden block with a cord, a hand, a modern mouse, a mouse (the animal) with a question mark.
- **Check:** SRI and the Computer History Museum agree on the wooden shell, the knife-edge wheels and the single button.

### 14. Google was first called BackRub
- **Hook:** "Did you know Google was originally called BackRub?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, गूगल का पहला नाम बैकरब था?"
- **Fact:** Larry Page and Sergey Brin started it in 1996 as a Stanford research project that analysed back links.
- **Beats:** They renamed it Google, a spelling of googol (1 followed by 100 zeros). The company was set up in 1998.
- **Stickers:** hands giving a back rub, a magnifying glass, a number 1 with many zeros, a university building.
- **Check:** Wikipedia and Stanford. The exact date of the rename is unclear, so avoid giving one.

### 15. Two pizzas cost 10,000 bitcoin
- **Hook:** "Did you know someone once paid 10,000 bitcoin for two pizzas?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, किसी ने कभी दो पिज़्ज़ा के लिए 10,000 बिटकॉइन दे दिए थे?"
- **Fact:** On May 22, 2010, programmer Laszlo Hanyecz paid 10,000 BTC for two pizzas, the first known real-world bitcoin purchase.
- **Beats:** The coins were worth about $41 then. May 22 is now "Bitcoin Pizza Day".
- **Stickers:** pizza, gold coin, a calendar, a pile of coins.
- **Check:** Bitcoin wiki and Guinness World Records. LIVE: look up the current price if you quote today's value.

### 16. The first computer "virus" only said hello
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first computer virus just displayed a taunt?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, पहला कंप्यूटर वायरस सिर्फ़ एक चुनौती भरा संदेश दिखाता था?"
- **Fact:** Creeper, written by Bob Thomas in 1971 for ARPANET, showed "I'M THE CREEPER: CATCH ME IF YOU CAN".
- **Beats:** It did no damage. Ray Tomlinson, the email pioneer, wrote Reaper in 1972 to hunt it down, often called the first antivirus.
- **Stickers:** a green creeping creature (generic), network cable, a scythe, a computer screen.
- **Check:** Wikipedia. Experts call Creeper a worm, so say "often called the first computer virus".

### 17. Bluetooth is named after a Viking king
- **Hook:** "Did you know Bluetooth is named after a Viking king?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, ब्लूटूथ का नाम एक वाइकिंग राजा के नाम पर पड़ा है?"
- **Fact:** Harald "Bluetooth" Gormsson united Denmark in the 900s. Intel's Jim Kardach proposed the name as a placeholder in 1997.
- **Beats:** The logo joins the runes for H and B. The name stuck because the alternative could not be trademarked.
- **Stickers:** viking helmet, crown, a rune stone, headphones.
- **Check:** Mental Floss, Gizmodo and TechXplore agree. Where the king's nickname came from is unknown, so do not explain it.

### 18. Nintendo started as a playing card company
- **Hook:** "Did you know Nintendo was making playing cards before video games existed?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, वीडियो गेम आने से पहले निंटेंडो ताश के पत्ते बनाती थी?"
- **Fact:** Fusajiro Yamauchi founded it in Kyoto on September 23, 1889, to make hand-painted hanafuda cards.
- **Beats:** That is over 130 years ago. Video games came in the 1970s.
- **Stickers:** hanafuda cards (flower cards), a game controller, a calendar, a Japanese house.
- **Check:** Wikipedia. Do not translate the name; the "leave luck to heaven" meaning is unverified.

### 19. The first text message said "Merry Christmas"
- **Hook:** "Did you know the first text message ever sent said 'Merry Christmas'?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, दुनिया का पहला टेक्स्ट मैसेज "मेरी क्रिसमस" था?"
- **Fact:** Neil Papworth sent it on December 3, 1992, to Vodafone director Richard Jarvis.
- **Beats:** Papworth typed it on a computer because phones had no keyboards for it. Jarvis read it on an Orbitel 901.
- **Stickers:** old phone, Christmas hat, speech bubble, computer keyboard.
- **Check:** Wikipedia and History.com. Say "the first text, according to Vodafone"; a test message may have come earlier.

### 20. Amazon was nearly called Cadabra
- **Hook:** "Did you know Amazon was almost called Cadabra?"
- **Hindi hook:** "क्या आप जानते हैं, अमेज़न का नाम लगभग कैडाब्रा रखा जाने वाला था?"
- **Fact:** Jeff Bezos first incorporated it as Cadabra, Inc. (from abracadabra) in 1994. His lawyer reportedly heard it as "cadaver".
- **Beats:** He renamed it after the Amazon River, the biggest river in the world, because he wanted the biggest bookstore.
- **Stickers:** magic hat and wand, a river, a book, a delivery box.
- **Check:** Brad Stone's *The Everything Store*, via Business Insider and Vator. Most sources give 1994 for the incorporation.
