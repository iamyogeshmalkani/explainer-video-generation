# When the Black Death hit England

> Sample facts, from standard histories (for example Britannica and the British Library): the plague reached {Melcombe|Mel come} Regis in Dorset in the summer of 1348; it spread along trade routes and was carried by fleas on rats; it reached London in the autumn of 1348; estimates say a third to a half of England's people died by about 1350; labour became scarce, wages rose, and the crown tried to cap them with the Ordinance of Labourers (1349) and the Statute of Labourers (1351). ## story {transition=cut hold=1.5}
[#s1] [loud]In the summer of 1348,[/loud] one ship docked at a small English port. [#s2] Nobody on the {quay|key} knew what it carried. [#s3] The plague had already crossed Europe along trade routes. [#s4] It travelled as fleas, on rats, aboard merchant ships. [#s5] In June, it reached {Melcombe|Mel come} Regis, in Dorset. [#s6] Within weeks, villagers had fevers, and painful swellings in the groin and armpit. [#s7] Many were dead within days. [#s8] By autumn, the plague had reached London. [#s9] Streets emptied, and the dead outnumbered the gravediggers. [#s10] Doctors had no cure, because nobody yet knew about germs. [#s11] By 1350, [loud]between a third and a half[/loud] of England's people had died. [#s12] Whole villages stood empty, and fields went unploughed. [#s13] Then something unexpected happened. [#s14] With so few workers left, the survivors could ask for higher pay. [#s15] The crown tried to freeze wages by law, but it barely worked. [#s16] The plague that destroyed so many also began to loosen the old feudal order.
