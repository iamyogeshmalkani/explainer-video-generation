# Deep Work: the book in one minute

> Facts: Deep Work: Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World, by Cal Newport, Grand Central Publishing, January 2016 (library catalogs; publisher listing). Newport defines deep work as focus without distraction on a cognitively demanding task; shallow work is logistical tasks done while distracted. His "deep work hypothesis": deep work is becoming rare and valuable at the same time. Attention residue comes from Sophie Leroy's 2009 paper on task switching (Wharton; Leroy 2009, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes). The four rules are Work Deeply, Embrace Boredom, Quit Social Media, Drain the Shallows (book summaries by Flinders University, ClickUp, tldv). Rule 1 offers scheduling philosophies; Newport uses Carl Jung's stone tower at Bollingen on Lake Zurich as an example, and Bill Gates's "Think Weeks" of isolated reading (reviews on Substack, Asana). Secondary sources say the 1995 Think Week produced the memo that turned Microsoft toward the internet; the memo name and exact date are left out. "Plan every minute of your day" is Newport's own rule 4 advice. The three takeaways are this video's reading, not quotes.
