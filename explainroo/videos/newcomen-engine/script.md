# The pump that started the age of steam

> Facts: Wikipedia "Newcomen atmospheric engine" and "Thomas Newcomen", ERIH biography, and "Watt steam engine". Newcomen was an ironmonger and Baptist lay preacher from Dartmouth. Thomas Savery's patent forced him into a partnership. An engine was working at the Conygree coal works near Dudley in 1712 (an earlier one near Wolverhampton is possible). Steam is condensed by a cold water spray, the partial vacuum lets air pressure push the piston, and the engine pumped water out of mines. The same cylinder was heated and cooled every stroke, which wasted heat. The patent expired in 1733 with more than 100 engines in Britain and abroad. James Watt, repairing a model Newcomen engine in Glasgow in 1765, added a separate condenser; Smeaton measured a Watt engine at about half the coal of a good Newcomen engine. Watt's saving let engines work away from the coal mines. The line that Newcomen engines suited coal mines because cheap coal made their appetite tolerable is the common explanation but comes from general knowledge, not a checked source.

## story {transition=cut hold=1.5}
[#s1] Before the Industrial Revolution, there was a flooded mine. [#s2] In the early 1700s, Britain needed coal. [#s3] But the deeper the miners dug, the more water seeped in. [#s4] Men hauled it out in buckets, [#s5] and horses walked in circles to turn the pumps. [#s6] It was never enough, and the mines kept flooding. [#s7] In Devon, an ironmonger named {Thomas Newcomen|Thomas New kuh mun} sold iron goods to miners. [#s8] They knew this problem better than anyone. [#s9] For years he worked on a better pump, [#s10] and his idea used steam. [#s11] Fill a cylinder with steam, then spray in cold water. [#s12] The steam turns back into water and leaves a vacuum. [#s13] The weight of the air pushes the piston down, [#s14] and the piston drives the pump. [#s15] In 1712, an engine like this was pumping at a coal works near Dudley. [#s16] It could work all day, without horses. [#s17] But there was a catch. [#s18] Every stroke heated the cylinder, then cooled it again. [#s19] So the engine burned huge amounts of coal. [#s20] Almost anywhere else, that would have cost too much. [#s21] But at a coal mine, the fuel was right there. [#s22] It worked best where coal was nearly free. [#s23] By 1733, more than a hundred were working in Britain and abroad. [#s24] Then in 1765, James Watt repaired a model of one. [#s25] He saw the wasted heat, and gave the engine a separate condenser. [#s26] It used about half the coal. [#s27] Now steam engines could work in factories, far from any mine. [#s28] A pump for flooded mines had started the age of steam.
