# Netflix Architecture

> The user's brief: "Define the whole Netflix architecture of the netflix app and there servers. Use the netflix colors if using anywhere." Accent is Netflix red #E50914 on near-black.
> Facts used: Netflix says "essentially everything before you hit play happens in AWS" and everything after play is served by its own CDN, Open Connect (about.netflix.com, "How Netflix works with ISPs around the globe"). Open Connect was announced in 2012, delivers 100% of Netflix video traffic, has servers in close to 1,000 locations, thousands of ISPs take part, Netflix streams to 190 countries, and files are pre-positioned on the servers during off-peak hours (same source). Netflix finished its seven-year move to AWS in early January 2016, a move that began after a 2008 database failure (about.netflix.com, "Completing the Netflix cloud migration"). Netflix ended 2024 with 302 million paid memberships (Q4 2024 shareholder letter, January 21, 2025). Zuul (the API gateway), Eureka, Cassandra and EVCache are Netflix's published open source backend components (Netflix OSS, Netflix TechBlog). Netflix engineers describe the backend as hundreds of microservices (Josh Evans, QCon 2016, "Mastering Chaos").

[#s13] That server is an [loud]Open Connect[/loud] box, Netflix's own hardware, [#s14] sitting inside your internet provider's network. [#s15] Netflix fills it with popular shows at night, when the lines are quiet. [#s16] Close to [#loc] 1,000 locations, [#isps] thousands of providers, [#countries] [loud]190 countries[/loud]. [#s17] That is how [loud]302 million[/loud] members press play without the cloud carrying a single frame. [#s18] The cloud handles everything before play.
