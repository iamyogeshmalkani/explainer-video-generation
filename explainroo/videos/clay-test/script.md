# Why does bread rise?

> Facts: yeast is a living fungus (a single-celled one) that feeds on sugars in the dough and gives off carbon dioxide gas; the gluten in kneaded dough is stretchy and traps the gas as bubbles; heat in the oven makes the trapped gas expand; the heat also stops the yeast and the crust sets. Sources: any baking-science text, e.g. King Arthur Baking "How yeast works".

## story {transition=cut hold=1.5}
[#s1] Why does bread rise? [#s2] The secret is a tiny living thing called yeast. [#s3] Yeast is a fungus, and it loves to eat the sugar in flour. [#s4] As it eats, it breathes out a gas called carbon dioxide. [#s5] The dough is stretchy, so it traps every bubble, [#s6] like a balloon that slowly fills up. [#s7] Then the oven gets hot, and the bubbles swell even more. [#s8] The heat stops the yeast, and the crust turns firm. [#s9] That is why bread is soft, and full of tiny holes.
