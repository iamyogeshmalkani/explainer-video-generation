# The battle fought without a single bullet

> Facts used (checked with web search, October 2026). On the night of 15 June 2020, Indian and Chinese soldiers clashed in the Galwan Valley in eastern Ladakh, near Patrol Point 14 on the Line of Actual Control, high in the Himalayas. A 1996 agreement between the two countries bars opening fire within two kilometres of the line (a 2005 agreement also restricts firearms during face-offs), and the Indian Army said no bullets were fired. Reports describe stones, iron rods and clubs studded with nails. Colonel B. Santosh Babu, commanding officer of 16 Bihar, led the Indian side and was killed. India announced 20 dead; many fell from the steep ground into the icy river, and the army said 17 of the 20 died of injuries in sub-zero temperatures. China said nothing about its losses until 19 February 2021, about eight months later, when it acknowledged four dead. It was the first deadly clash on the India-China border since 1975, when four Indian soldiers were killed in an ambush in Arunachal Pradesh. Both countries have nuclear weapons. Unverified figures (Chinese casualty estimates, troop numbers, altitude) are left out. Sources: Tribune India, The Quint, Hindustan Times, Scroll.in, PBS, Morung Express, Factly.
