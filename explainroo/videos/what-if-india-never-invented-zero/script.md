# What if India never invented zero?

> Facts: Brahmagupta, 628 CE, gave rules for zero as a number. Other cultures used a gap or dot as a placeholder.

## story {transition=cut hold=1.2}
[loud]What if India never gave the world zero?[/loud] [#circle] Zero is just a circle. But try writing one thousand without it. [#brahma] Around fourteen hundred years ago, Indian mathematicians like {Brahmagupta|Brahma-goop-ta} wrote the rules for zero as a real number. [#gap] Other cultures used a gap or a dot as a placeholder. [#calc] India turned it into something you can add, subtract and calculate with. [#roman] Without it, we might be stuck with systems like Roman numerals. Try multiplying {XLVII|forty seven} by {CXXIII|one hundred twenty three}. [#modern] Algebra, modern mathematics and computers all lean on zero. Every phone, every app, every {UPI|U P I} payment is just ones and zeros. [#later] Someone else might have found it later. Maybe much later. Historians can only guess. [#end] Nothing, it turns out, was everything. So what if India never invented zero?
