# The air conditioner that was built for paper

> Facts: Wikipedia "Willis Carrier", Carrier company history (promotional, so only the basics are used), EIA Today in Energy 2022 (RECS 2020), and a transport-geography summary of Census data. In July 1902 Willis Carrier, a young engineer at Buffalo Forge, drew the system now seen as the first modern air conditioner for the Sackett-Wilhelms printing plant in Brooklyn, where humidity made the paper swell and shrink and spoiled multicolor printing. His idea was to run cold water through the coils instead of steam so the air reached a chosen dew point. Census: 58% of US homes had air conditioning in 1980 (about 75% in the southern divisions). EIA 2020: 88% of US households use it. From 1960 to 1980 the South's population and jobs grew faster than the North's. That the machine "helped" change where Americans live is a fair summary, not a proven single cause.

## story {transition=cut hold=1.5}
[#s1] In 1902, a printing plant in Brooklyn had a problem with the weather. [#s2] On humid days, the paper swelled, and the colored inks printed out of place. [#s3] A company called Buffalo Forge sent a young engineer named Willis Carrier. [#s4] He did not set out to cool the room. [#s5] He wanted to control the moisture in the air. [#s6] So he ran cold water through coils, and passed the air across them. [#s7] Moisture in the air condensed on the cold pipes, and the paper stopped swelling. [#s8] It is seen as the first modern air conditioner, and it was built for paper, not people. [#s9] Soon theaters, offices and homes wanted the cool air too. [#s10] By 1980, more than half of American homes had it, and today it is close to nine in ten. [#s11] From 1960 to 1980, the hot South also grew faster than the North. [#s12] A machine built to keep paper flat had helped change where Americans live.
