# Seven days of shells, and the wire still stood

> Facts used (Battle of the Somme, northern France, 1 July to 18 November 1916). The British preliminary bombardment began on 24 June 1916 and lasted seven days, firing more than 1.5 million shells (Imperial War Museums). British commanders expected it to destroy the German defences and cut the wire; in many places the wire was still largely intact and the defenders sat out the shelling in deep dugouts (Veterans Affairs Canada, Royal Hampshire Regiment). The Hawthorn Ridge mine was fired at 7:20 am on 1 July, ten minutes before zero hour at 7:30 am, which warned the Germans (Wikipedia, Keele University). British casualties on 1 July: 57,470, of whom 19,240 died (IWM), the worst day in British Army history. The battle ended on 18 November 1916 with more than one million casualties on all sides (IWM). Sir Douglas Haig commanded the British Expeditionary Force. Numbers that sources disagree on (shells beyond 1.5 million, ground gained) are left out.
