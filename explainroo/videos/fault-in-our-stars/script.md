# The Fault in Our Stars: book summary

> Facts: Wikipedia "The Fault in Our Stars" (the article is not fully sourced) for the plot. John Green's fourth solo novel, published 10 January 2012. Narrator Hazel Grace Lancaster is 16 and has thyroid cancer that has affected her lungs. At a support group she meets Augustus Waters, 17, whose osteosarcoma cost him a leg and is in remission. They swap books; Hazel's favourite, An Imperial Affliction, ends abruptly; its author Peter Van Houten lives in Amsterdam. Augustus arranges a trip through "The Genie Foundation" (the story's Make-A-Wish); Van Houten turns out to be a mean-spirited alcoholic; Hazel and Augustus share their first kiss at the Anne Frank House; the next day Augustus reveals his cancer has returned; he dies soon after; Hazel later finds a eulogy he wrote for her, saying getting hurt is unavoidable but we choose whom we let hurt us; the book closes with Hazel happy with her choice. Title: Julius Caesar, Act 1 Scene 2, "The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars". The three takeaways are this video's own reading, not quotes. The pictures avoid close faces and romance because the characters are teenagers.

## story {transition=cut hold=1.5}
[#s1] The Fault in Our Stars is a novel by John Green, [#s1b] published in January 2012. [#s2] Hazel is sixteen, and has thyroid cancer that has reached her lungs. [#s3] At a cancer support group, she meets Augustus Waters. [#s4] He lost a leg to bone cancer, but he is in remission. [#s5] They swap books, and they fall in love. [#s6] Hazel's favorite novel, An Imperial Affliction, ends abruptly. [#s7] So Augustus arranges a trip to Amsterdam, to meet its author. [#s8] But the author turns out to be a cruel, drunken man. [#s9] Still, at the Anne Frank House, they share their first kiss. [#s10] The next day, Augustus tells her his cancer has returned. [#s11] He dies soon after. [#s12] Later, Hazel finds a eulogy he wrote for her. [#s13] It says that hurt is unavoidable, but we get to choose who hurts us. [#s14] The title comes from Shakespeare's Julius Caesar: [#s15] "The fault, dear Brutus, is not in our stars." [#t1] First, love can be real, even when time is short. [#t2] Second, hurt is unavoidable, but we choose who we let hurt us. [#t3] And Hazel ends the book happy with her choice.
