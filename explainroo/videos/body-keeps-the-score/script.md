# The Body Keeps the Score: book summary

> Facts: Wikipedia "The Body Keeps the Score" (the article is not fully sourced). Bessel van der Kolk, Viking Press, 25 September 2014, based on his 1994 Harvard Review of Psychiatry article "The body keeps the score: memory and the evolving psychobiology of posttraumatic stress". It covers the purported effects of trauma on mind and body, stresses the attachment system and social environment as protection, and discusses EMDR, yoga and limbic system therapy. Bestseller for many years, 36 languages, nearly 300 weeks on the New York Times list per a 2024 Financial Times article. Criticism: a 2023 Washington Post review said it promotes "uncertain science"; a 2023 editorial (Cox and Codd) said it promotes treatments with limited or no evidence; a 2025 BJPsych Bulletin review of 122 claims found the claims about trauma-induced brain damage and the unique efficacy of body-based treatments are not supported by current research. The takeaways are this video's own reading, not quotes. The last takeaway points to qualified help because the topic is trauma.

## story {transition=cut hold=1.5}
[#s1] The Body Keeps the Score is a book by Bessel van der Kolk, published in 2014. [#s2] It is about how traumatic stress affects the mind and the body. [#s3] He argues that trauma leaves its mark on the body, not just in memory. [#s4] The book grew out of his 1994 article of the same name. [#s5] He stresses that close relationships protect people from trauma-related problems. [#s6] And he describes healing approaches, including {EMDR|E M D R}, and yoga. [#s7] The book became a huge bestseller. [#s8] It spent nearly three hundred weeks on the New York Times list. [#s9] But scientists have pushed back. [#s10] One review said it promotes uncertain science. [#s11] A 2025 review of one hundred twenty two of its claims [#s11b] found that two key ones are not supported by current research. [#s12] And an editorial said it promotes treatments with limited or no evidence. [#t1] First, van der Kolk argues that trauma marks the body as well as the mind. [#t2] Second, he points to safe relationships, {EMDR|E M D R} and yoga as ways to heal. [#t3] And critics say the science is shaky, so seek qualified help.
