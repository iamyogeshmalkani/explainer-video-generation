# The Great Fire of London

> Facts: the fire began in the early hours of Sunday 2 September 1666 at Thomas Farriner's bakery on (or just off) Pudding Lane; the likely cause was sparks from his oven, which was left with a fire burning for the night (London Museum, History Hit, BBC/History.co.uk summaries). The summer had been dry and the wooden houses stood close together; shops on Pudding Lane stored tar, rope and oil. Samuel Pepys carried news to King Charles II and urged that houses be pulled down (Pepys's diary, National Archives extract); Charles put his brother James, Duke of York, in charge, and crews pulled houses down to make firebreaks. The fire was stopped about four days after it began (contained by the Wednesday morning). London Museum: the fire destroyed about four fifths of the city and left around 100,000 people homeless. Recorded deaths were very few (the National Archives says nine; other sources say fewer), so the script says only "very few". The Rebuilding of London Act (passed February 1667) required new buildings to be of brick or stone instead of timber (UK Parliament; legislation.gov.uk 18 & 19 Cha. 2 c. 8). Not used because sources disagree or are weak: exact death toll, the Lord Mayor "a woman might piss it out" story, the number of houses and churches.
